Sounds of the Summer

A collection of songs for the summer, temporally collected from near and far. Those from far are capturing nostalgia largely, of younger summers and gentler days. There is very little that is gentle about this summer, one so putridly hot that my skin has been melting since June, and I am more aware than ever of the impending climate catastrophe that we are rubbing shoulders with: a decline of our own design. I think it is fair to say that I am not a summer person, much preferring the cool of Autumn or blossoming possibility of Spring, but in these songs I try to anchor a particular feeling, of a break from the mundane, the long summer holidays during schooltime, or the briefly captured week of annual leave in mid-August.

‘Alive’ – Empire of the Sun

The most recent addition on my list, this is a song for the peak of carefree summer days. As with the majority of these songs, it’s a windows down, hair flying, letting go sort of wonder. On the flip-side, I also think it captures for me a certain type of mania, of marching into the abyss with a heavy beat and a beaming smile.

‘She’s Casual’ – The Hunna

A purely nostalgic song, this is the sound of seventeen to me. I associate this song largely with summers in Cornwall, walking along the breakwater and quiet canals before packing up my clothes for a trip to a local festival. It starts with a soft introduction and leaps into a crazy, heart-bending summer anthem.

‘What For’ – Sea Girls

It’s not totally accurate for me to call this a summer song. It followed me through several years of my teenagerhood and is one of the earliest singles by the indie-rock band Sea Girls. Sea Girls are my ‘I knew them before I was famous’ moment, as a huge fan since sixteen, I have followed their output from their earliest EPs. I chose this song for the list because it evokes the feeling of a relentless sun on your face, almost running like a live performance, as if you are listening from the pit at a festival, surviving purely off stall-bought noodles and water from the fountain by the toilets.

‘Cough Syrup’ – Young the Giant

The day is coming to a close, the heat dissipating, and this song is playing through some tinny speakers on the cooling sand. You have a can of cider in one hand and the swathes of the ocean in front of you. As you listen, you become perpetually seventeen.

‘Bad Guys Always Win’ – Circa Waves

I associate this fast-paced, dizzy song with early summer days in Wales, staying in a bed and breakfast, waking each day to the eager sun crawling its way underneath the curtains. It’s a short and snappy track on Circa Waves’ Death & Love. Overall, this entire album is a slice of summer for me, largely for periods of intense listening in the early summer months. Unlike some of the songs on this list, that for me capture the high days of heat in July or the slow decay of green that heralds the beginning of Autumn, this is a song for the start of summer, when Spring gives way and collapses into endless open fields.

‘Feeding Line’ – Boy & Bear

Another track for a late evening walk, when the days are at their longest and it doesn’t get dark until ten. This song has been floating around my head for a while, linked to the endless paths around my house, the grassy verges leaking their green in the heat, the fields wrapped up and pressed into neat hay bales, the wizening trees and sweating, burning sky. It’s my walk home from school and my post-university nature loop.

‘Girls Like You’ – The Naked and Famous

You have to commit to this song. It’s a bit on the longer side, walking steadily with an almost pestering consistency, before it leaps seamlessly to suffocating, tantalising rhythm. Whenever I listen to this song, I feel as if I am trapped inside an American blockbuster, at the edge of a canyon or over a vast lake, young and carefree, my whole life whittled down to those moments of childish wonder. With a long outro, the day in time fades away, and you are left to ponder the fleeting intensity of your youth.

‘Inbetween Days’ – The Cure

A classic for a reason, the soundtrack to a coming-of-age film, a great song for the train (from which I am writing this now), where you can watch the endless English fields sprint past you with magnetising sharpness, like light from the slats of your blinds.

‘Getaway’ – Blossoms

A hypnotic summer beat, the sort of song you can play when you are getting ready in the morning for a day in the lethargic sun. Once again, this is a song pulled from my youth. There is certainly a melancholy underneath the upbeat rhythm, one which I can align with the feeling of time running slowly away from you, and the cold of autumn bracing for your impact.

‘I Dare You’ – The Regrettes

Completely feel-good fun. I have loved this song for a while, and it captures for me the heady abandon that accompanies having a summer crush or even falling in love. The Regrettes have a knack for this kind of music, as if you stumble on the diary of an energetic young woman who has an unquenchable lust for life. It’s great music to listen to when you need an emotional pick-me-up.

‘Riptide’ – Vance Joy

The quintessential summer song, only slightly dampened by its constant replay value in retail corporate playlists. There is a certain freedom to this undeniable hit, a sense of possibility that got me through many tough days working in retail. It was the song that came on and lifted a little of the white-light headache and customer service fatigue. I could picture myself bursting out of the automatic doors onto the wet sand of my favourite beach. It still has that same power of escapism.

‘You Only Live Once’ – The Strokes

This is one of my favourite songs by The Strokes, and that is a very difficult choice to make. There are few other artists that can make you feel cool in the way The Strokes can, like sunglasses, open-top car down an open road in the summer heat.

‘End of August’ – Noah Kahan

A fitting song to end the list, ‘End of August’ is a homage to the end of a long summer, the twilight days when the warmth is behind you, the fields are the colour of straw and the last lazy days of August are buried underneath suncream and sprinklers. There is something beautifully melancholy about this song, as it ushers in not only autumn but a premature winter. Against the background of cicadas, you can imagine yourself sat on a flimsy, dirty plastic chair as the sun sets, the bugs swarming around your ankles, a drink in hand, the heat finally giving way to the temperamental evening breeze. Already, you can feel the promise of another summer, another year, another love, another life.
